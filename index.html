Epistemological Position

Interpretivism: politics as meaning-making

01

Meaning

Political roles are treated as meanings people construct rather than fixed categories with one objective definition.

02

Context

Interpretations can depend on the candidate, setting and participant’s own understandings of politics and representation.

03

Reasoning

Participants’ explanations are treated as evidence of how they understand political roles.

09 / 17

Literature Review

What do we already know?

Political outsiders

Knecht & Rosentrater (2021)

Celebrity candidates are one type of political outsider, but existing research focuses mainly on electoral performance and political entry rather than how citizens define political experience.

Political legitimacy

Beta & Neyazi (2022) · Street (2024)

Public figures can turn visibility, popularity, representation, expertise and experience into political legitimacy, but this legitimacy has to be recognised rather than automatically transferred from fame.

Background & authenticity

Anceschi & Silvan (2026) · Harff & Schmuck (2024)

Professional background, public identity and perceived authenticity can shape how political figures and their messages are interpreted.

Existing research explains political entry, legitimacy and authenticity — but less about what political roles people assign to celebrity candidates.

Expectations

What does the literature lead me to expect?

01

Political experience

Participants treat political experience as an marker of the political roles candidates can credibly perform.

02

Representation & expertise

Participants associate political roles with the ability to represent interests and contribute through professional or political expertise.

03

Authenticity & prior identity

Participants interpret political roles through candidates’ previous public identities and perceived authenticity.

Interpretivist expectations, not hypotheses to statistically test.

Method

Making political roles visible

Candidate card sortingParticipants categorise candidates with different backgrounds.
↓
Semi-structured interviewsParticipants explain why they placed candidates where they did.
↓
Interpretive analysisTheir explanations reveal how they understand political roles.

The cards structure the conversation — participants’ explanations guide the interpretation.

The aim is not to force people into predetermined categories, but to make their own meanings and expectations visible.
